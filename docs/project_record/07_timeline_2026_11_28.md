## 7.1 Why the old schedule was wrong, and in which direction

It assumed the build was the long pole. It was not. Two things turned out to dominate instead,
and both were measured rather than guessed:

| Assumption in the design document | Measured, 2026-11-28 |
|---|---|
| "Each evaluation is microseconds, so T4 and T5 are trivial" | One patient at the registered budget of 60, GP-EI plus random search: **33.3 s**. The objective *is* microseconds; the GP surrogate refit is not. |
| 3 to 5 weeks of analyst time for the module, tests and dry run | Done, with the Emax literature base built from scratch on top of it |

The second is why the schedule collapsed; the first is why one part of it must now **expand**.

## 7.3 The one thing that got bigger: the sensitivity plan

`docs/t4t5_model_design.md` §5 proposes a **1000-draw Latin hypercube** plus a tornado over
~20 parameters. Whether that is trivial or impossible turns entirely on one question nobody
asked: **does each draw re-run the optimiser, or re-score the configuration already chosen?**

Measured, for 268 patients (T4's 50 plus T5's 218):

| | Re-score | Re-optimise |
|---|---|---|
| One patient, one setting | 148 µs | 33.3 s (**225,000×**) |
| Tornado, ~40 settings | 1.6 s | 99 h |
| Latin hypercube, 1000 draws | **0.7 min** | **2,480 h — 103 days** |

So the plan as written is either a minute's work or a quarter of a year, and the document does
not say which. It needs to be split before it is run:

* **Tier 1, re-score, 1000 draws (~1 min).** Hold each patient's chosen configuration fixed at
  what the central parameters chose, and re-score it under each draw. This answers *"how much
  does the predicted benefit of the chosen therapy depend on the assumptions?"* — which is the
  question a reader of the paper has.
* **Tier 2, re-optimise, a handful of settings (~1 h for 10).** Answers the different question
  *"under these assumptions, would the optimiser have chosen a different therapy at all?"*
  Needed for T4's distinct-configuration count and regret reduction, which are properties of
  the optimiser and cannot be re-scored. Scope it to the parameters tier 1 shows matter, plus
  the prior regimes of §5 item 4.

This is a change to a **working plan, not to registered text**: item 11 adopted Design A's
model, and §3.6 registers that exploratory analyses exist, but neither registers the
sensitivity plan's internals. No amendment is needed. It does need writing down before the
analyses run, so that the split is a design choice and not a retrofit around a slow script.

## 7.5 What this says about how the estimates were made

Worth recording, because the same mistake is easy to repeat:

1. **The build estimate was ~10× too long.** "3 to 5 weeks for one analyst" was a reasonable
   human estimate and a bad estimate for this process. Work that parallelises across
   independent readers — screening 108 records, extracting 40 papers — compressed hardest.
2. **The compute estimate was ~5 orders of magnitude too optimistic**, in the one place it
   mattered, because it costed the objective instead of the surrogate around it. The
   measurement took two minutes and should have been made when the sentence was written.
3. **The two errors are not symmetric.** Being early on the build cost nothing. Being wrong
   about compute would have been discovered only when a sensitivity analysis failed to finish,
   after the freeze, with the registered deadline approaching.

The rule this suggests: **estimate human work by analogy, and machine work by measurement** —
and when a plan says something is "trivial", time it.
